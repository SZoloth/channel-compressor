## The combined doctrine

1. **Separate building from product work.** Faster output is useful, but output is not an outcome.
2. **Build to learn before building to earn.** State the customer and business result, then use cheap prototypes to make contact with reality.
3. **Preserve the learning loop.** Automate repeatable known work; keep people close to customers, decisions, critique and consequences.
4. **Manage convictions, not feature inventories.** Define the future you believe in, the evidence that would support or falsify it, and what earns more investment.
5. **Make commitment explicit.** Label work as a disposable probe, a sustained experiment, or a durable customer promise.
6. **Treat context as infrastructure.** Agents need product principles, customer evidence, design systems, prior decisions and ownership—not a longer generic prompt.
7. **Install an editor.** When generating options is cheap, rejection, integration and end-to-end judgment become scarcer.
8. **Use AI to increase ambition.** The compelling prize is not more middling features; it is experiments and experiences that were previously uneconomic.

## Put it into practice

Convert one active initiative from a feature roadmap into this compact decision record:

```text
Conviction: What future or customer change do we believe in?
Evidence: What observation would support it?
Falsifier: What would make us stop or revise it?
Probe: What is the cheapest real-world test?
Commitment: Probe, experiment, or promise?
Context: Which customer evidence, principles and prior decisions must people and agents see?
Editor: Who judges the end-to-end result and can reject “done” work?
```

Then run a short feature roast: show the experience without defending it, collect raw points
of confusion, and synthesize them afterward. This is the highest-leverage behavior shared by
the four talks.
