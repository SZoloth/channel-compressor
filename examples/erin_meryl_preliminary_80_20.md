# Erin Meryl — preliminary 80/20 map

**Status:** directional, based on a transcript sample rather than the completed channel-wide run. The executable pipeline in this repository is the mechanism for the definitive result.

## The dominant repetition pattern

Across the sampled videos, most advice reduces to six durable ideas:

1. **Replace passive consumption with production or retrieval.** Explain, write, record, map, or answer from memory.
2. **Practice at the edge of current ability and inspect errors.** Repetition matters most when feedback changes the next repetition.
3. **Turn curiosity into a personal curriculum.** Define domains, research questions, reputable inputs, outputs, calendar time, and a minimum viable session.
4. **Lower cheap-input noise.** Remove compulsive scrolling and deliberately tolerate low stimulation so long-form attention has a chance.
5. **Shrink aspirations into a concrete next action.** Build consistency with realistic commitments; recover quickly from misses instead of moralizing them.
6. **Protect the substrate.** Sleep and exercise support learning, but the channel often states the underlying neuroscience more confidently than the transcript evidence warrants.

## Compress rather than watch

- **How to Actually Get Smarter:** useful checklist—deliberate practice, novel skills, exercise, sleep, and close reading—but mostly recombines the queue above.
- **how to be ADDICTED to hard work:** preserve “tighten the effort–reward loop” and progressive difficulty; discount the over-neat dopamine explanation.
- **you are addicted to the potential that you will never fulfil:** preserve “shrink the goal and define the first action”; the remainder substantially overlaps discipline and attention.
- **you need an intellectual glow up:** largely a less operational precursor to the personal-curriculum and production videos.
- **your friends will be so jealous of how effectively you'll be able to study:** comprehensive but heavily exam-oriented and redundant for Sam.
- **You Have More Control Over Your Life Than You Think:** broad interview treatment of agency, hard work, and multiple goals; useful as conversation, not as the most efficient instructional source.

## The practical synthesis

A better outcome than bingeing the channel is to run this four-week loop:

1. Choose one domain and one narrow research question.
2. Select one reputable primary resource and one accessible secondary resource.
3. Schedule three small sessions, each ending in an output from memory.
4. Review the output, identify one weakness, and change one variable in the next repetition.
5. Keep the phone out of the session; let mild boredom remain mild boredom.
6. At week’s end, publish or explain the result and decide whether the next module compounds the same spine or adds a deliberate wildcard.

That loop captures most of the channel’s repeated value while turning the account from entertainment into an instrument.
